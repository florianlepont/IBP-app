<objective>
Run the fine-tuned model on the two real benchmark phones and record inference latency against the
3 s budget, then validate accuracy a second time on field photographs — the half of D-16 that
decides whether the public-dataset figures survive contact with real conditions.

Purpose: D-05 requires inference under three seconds on the measured devices, and D-18 requires
those devices to be the phones the observers actually carry. RESEARCH.md Pitfall 4 is blunt about
what goes wrong otherwise: a simulator or a flagship test phone reports a comfortable number and
hides a real-world failure. D-16 is equally blunt about the second step: lab figures routinely
collapse under real conditions, and validating on field photographs is what makes the go/no-go
honest.

Output: device latency and field-photo accuracy as recorded numbers, and a complete measurement
document with sections 6, 7 and 8 filled.

Timebox discipline (D-19): this is the last measuring plan. If the field photographs did not
arrive, or the timebox runs out, record exactly what was and was not measured and hand that to the
ADR. A partial measurement honestly reported is a legitimate input to a no-go. A well-evidenced
no-go is a successful outcome for this phase.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| spike host → personal handset | An unsigned development build is sideloaded onto a developer-owned or association-owned phone |
| field photographs → spike tree | Photographs supplied by the association may carry GPS EXIF and identifiable locations, and are copied onto the benchmark handsets |
| measurement → decision | A figure recorded without its device, its sample count or its network state misdirects the go/no-go irreversibly |
