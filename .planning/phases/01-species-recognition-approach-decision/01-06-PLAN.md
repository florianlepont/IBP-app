    meaningless. Date it today.

    Read the measurement document first and in full. Every figure in this ADR is transcribed from it
    with the section cited. Do not project, round up, or restate a validation figure as a test
    figure. RESEARCH.md's Sampling Rate gate is explicit: the ADR is not accepted until it cites
    specific recorded numbers.

    Then read the four gate files, because they tell you which branch of this plan you are in:
    `spike/species-recognition/data/GATE` (corpus),
    `spike/species-recognition/device-harness/GATE` (harness and runtime),
    `spike/species-recognition/eval/GATE` (model) and
    `spike/species-recognition/eval/GATE-MEASURE` (measurement).

    **This ADR is written and ratified in every branch, including every early exit.** If an upstream
    gate stopped the spike — no usable corpus, a harness that would not build, a model never trained
    — the phase's question is still answered, and the answer is a no-go with a documented cause. An
    early exit that never reaches a ratified ADR would leave the phase unfinished and REQ-ML-adr
    unmet, which is the one outcome worse than a no-go. Write the ADR as a no-go, state which gate
    stopped the spike and quote its token and reason, and say what was and was not measured. The
    Measured evidence section then carries the coverage and gate findings that exist rather than
    accuracy and latency tables that do not — cite what there is, and be explicit about what is
    absent and why.

    **Runtime substitution.** Read the `RUNTIME:` and `RUNTIME-SUBSTITUTION:` lines from the harness
    gate. If the flag reads `yes`, the Decision section's runtime bullet must name
    `onnxruntime-react-native` *and* state in the same bullet that this is a deviation from
    RESEARCH.md's primary recommendation of `react-native-fast-tflite`, with the reason from section
    8. An ADR that names the fallback runtime without flagging the deviation reads as though the
    research recommended it, which would mislead Phase 3 into treating a worked-around choice as a
    researched one. If the flag reads `no`, say so plainly too — that the primary recommendation
    held is itself worth recording.

    Sections to write:

    **Status / Date** — as above.

    **Context.** US-C9 (`docs/specs/epic-c-ibp-survey-data-entry.md`) asks the app to suggest tree
    species from a photograph. `.planning/INGEST-CONFLICTS.md` warning 5 records that it entered the
    MVP with zero stack, architecture or contract coverage, which is why this phase exists. Name
    what was measured and over what period, against the two-to-three day timebox (D-19).

    **Decision.** The go, partial go or no-go for US-C9 in this milestone, stated in the first line
    of the section, in one sentence, before any qualification. Then, as bullets in the ADR-001
    style, the technical decisions the measurement supports:
    - Recognition targets the genus and never the species (D-01), because Factor A counts native
      genera from the closed CNPF list — 34 classes, sourced from the official CNPF document and
      recorded in section 2.
    - The success bar is the correct genus within the top three candidates, ninety-five per cent of
      the time, reported per genus and never as a single average (D-02, D-03).
    - Inference runs on-device only, with no server inference and no network fallback (D-06), which
      follows from ADR-001's locked offline-first decision DEC-006 and from the photograph being
      taken in a forest with no signal. Cite the completed airplane-mode pass from section 7 as the
      checked proof, not as an intention.
    - The inference runtime, named with its version and licence, from section 8.
    - The model: backbone, training corpus, licence chain from architecture through pretrained
      weights to training images (D-09), and its on-disk size in bytes from section 4.
    - The model is downloaded separately on first launch and not bundled in the binary (D-07),
      served from the existing S3/MinIO object storage over TLS.
    - Suggestions are presented most-likely-genus-first with the remaining candidates underneath
      (D-11), with confidence in plain words — strong, medium, weak — and never as a percentage
      (D-12). Give the proposed band thresholds and cite the confidence distribution in section 5
      they were derived from, so a later reader can see they were measured rather than chosen.
    - The recognition photograph is transient and is not kept (D-13): it does not consume the
      ten-photo attachment quota, costs no storage or sync, and requires no data-contract extension.

    **Measured evidence.** A compact table of the figures that carry the decision, each with the
    measurement-document section it comes from: per-genus top-3 accuracy on the public held-out
    split and on field photographs, how many genera clear the bar and how many cannot be assessed,
    model size in bytes, and median and worst-case total latency per real device against the
    three-second budget (D-05). Name both devices with their OS versions — a latency figure without
    a device attached is not a measurement (RESEARCH.md Pitfall 4). Do not reproduce all 34 rows
    here; link to section 5 and give the summary counts.

    **If the decision is a partial go (D-04).** List by name the genera for which suggestions are
    enabled, transcribed from plan 04's clears-bar column — do not re-derive them. State that manual
    entry stays fully usable for every other genus, so the ecologist gains time where recognition is
    reliable and is never misled where it is not. State how the remaining genera are treated in the
    interface: they are simply not suggested, not suggested-with-a-warning.

    **If the decision is a no-go.** State the fallback explicitly: Factor A stays manual entry, which
    is what the app does today and what the form is designed around. State what moves to the next
    milestone. State what would have to change for the answer to be different — the corpus, the
    device budget, the timebox — so the next attempt starts from evidence rather than from scratch.
    A no-go here is a successful outcome of this phase: it cost days rather than months, which is
    exactly what the ROADMAP says the gate is for.

    **Behaviour when the model is unavailable.** ROADMAP criterion 1 requires this and D-08 fixes it:
    when the model is not yet downloaded or fails to load, the app shows an explicit message and
    falls back to normal manual entry. Silent fallback is rejected, because the ecologist would
    conclude the feature is broken. Cite the missing-model pass from section 7 as evidence that the
    behaviour was actually exercised on both handsets.

    **Model distribution integrity.** The downloaded model file is untrusted input to a native
    inference runtime. Require Phase 3 to verify the downloaded file's checksum against a manifest
    value before loading it, to serve it over TLS from the existing object-storage endpoint, and to
    fail closed to the D-08 explicit-message path on mismatch or load failure rather than crashing.
    This is RESEARCH.md's security-domain control and it belongs in the ADR because Phase 3 will
    implement it from here.

    **Cost.** ROADMAP criterion 4. On-device inference adds no recurring inference cost to the
    ~€346/yr running budget recorded in `.planning/PROJECT.md` — by construction, since no inference
    crosses the network (D-06), evidenced by the airplane-mode pass. State the one-off and marginal
    costs that do exist and are not zero: hosting the model file on the object storage already paid
    for, its egress on first launch per install, and the one-off effort of training or retraining.
    Give the model size and a plain arithmetic sketch of the egress at a realistic install count for
    an internal-only milestone. If any figure would be a recurring cost, state it plainly rather
    than rounding it to nothing — the criterion permits a stated cost, it does not require zero.

    **Preconditions (D-15).** Record the discovered Factor A data-model gap as a precondition of
    US-C9. The shipped app stores Factor A as a single number, `native_genus_count`
    (`mobile/src/app/constants.ts`, `mobile/src/storage/db.ts`,
    `api/src/surveys/ibp-rules.service.ts`), while `docs/specs/ibp-form-spec.md` §Factor A specifies
    a multi-select list of observed native genera with a derived counter. The consequence is direct
    and must be stated in those terms: recognition produces a genus name and the form has nowhere to
    put it, so even a perfect model delivers nothing until the form matches the spec. The work —
    data contract, API, IBP scoring on both client and server, the mobile screen, and a migration
    for surveys already recorded as a bare count, which cannot be decomposed back into named genera
    — belongs to Phase 2. Note that the 17 reference cases in
    `docs/technical/ibp-validation-matrix-v1.md` must keep passing through that change.

    **What is out of reach for this milestone.** Two items, both settled with evidence rather than
    dropped.
    - The stand-photo case: recognising several trees in one photograph of a stand. D-10 was amended
      on 2026-09-22 after research to scope this phase to single-subject photographs only. Record
      why: it requires object detection before classification, and no licence-clean, CNPF-genus
      detector or dataset exists. Cite the two nearest candidates by name and by their specific
      disqualification — SilvaScenes, which covers Quebec species and whose licence RESEARCH.md
      could not confirm from the repository itself (assumption A3), and ForTrunkDet, which is CC-BY
      but covers only Eucalyptus and Pinus. Building a detector from scratch is a multi-week effort
      with its own data-acquisition problem. State that no stand-case figure exists in the
      measurement document, and that this is deliberate: a whole-image classifier pointed at a stand
      photograph returns a confident answer about whichever trunk dominates the frame, which looks
      like evidence and is not (RESEARCH.md Pitfall 3).
    - Capturing the ecologist's corrections of wrong suggestions to build an improvement dataset
      (D-14). It needs storage, a GDPR position and a retraining story — a capability of its own,
      belonging to a later milestone.

    **Out of Scope for This Decision (later).** Following ADR-001's section of the same name:
    species-level identification beyond genus (D-01 scopes this phase to genus because that is what
    Factor A counts); server-side recognition over already-synced photographs, which is a different
    capability with a different cost profile and is rejected here only for field use; and the choice
    of which CNPF methodology version the app follows — RESEARCH.md Open Question 1 records that
    this repository cites IBP Fr v3.0 while CNPF publishes v3.2, and that resolving it belongs to
    Phase 2, which owns the genus list as a real entity.

    **Alternatives considered.** Brief, in ADR-001's register. The second-choice runtime and why it
    was not used. `react-native-executorch`, which RESEARCH.md's Package Legitimacy Audit flags SUS
    on publish-recency grounds (version 0.10.2, published 2026-09-11) despite a credible maintainer
    — recorded as deliberately avoided so the phase needed no legitimacy exception. Writing directly
    against Core ML and NNAPI, which doubles the native surface. Server-hosted inference, ruled out
    categorically by D-06.

    **Hand-off to Phases 2 and 3.** Explicit, in both branches, because the ROADMAP gates both
    phases on this decision.
    - On a go or a partial go: what Phase 2 receives — the 34-class genus list with its CNPF source
      and version, the confirmed model and runtime, the confidence bands, and the D-15 data-model
      work it must complete before Phase 3 can use any of it. What Phase 3 receives — the model
      distribution and integrity requirements, the D-08 unavailable behaviour, the D-11/D-12
      presentation rules, the D-13 transient-photo rule, and the integration cost from section 8.
      On a partial go, also the enabled-genus list, which Phase 3 must enforce rather than suggest
      across all 34 classes.
    - On a no-go: Phases 2 and 3 fall away as the ROADMAP already anticipates, and
      REQ-C-species-recognition moves to the next milestone. Note that REQ-DOC-form-spec and the
      D-15 Factor A correction are worth doing regardless — the form diverges from its own
      specification whether or not recognition ships — and that the ROADMAP already records Phases
      4, 5 and 6 as independent of this track and reorderable ahead of it.

    **Expected Validation.** An ADR-001-style checklist, unticked, naming what would confirm this
    decision in the field: the per-genus figures holding up during the Phase 7 field tests, latency
    holding on the observers' actual handsets, and the D-08 unavailable path behaving as described
    when a real first launch happens without Wi-Fi.

    Two things to hold to throughout. Every number carries its measurement-document section. And the
    ADR states its own confidence honestly — section 9's caveats are inherited here, not left

    small sample counts, and RESEARCH.md's A4 caveat that the CC-BY licence reading is a documented
    reading and not legal advice and must be re-verified before the model ships.
